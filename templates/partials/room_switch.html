{% comment %} The room switcher — one control, every page, both surfaces. A group has its own ladder, its own wall and its own tips, and they are genuinely different data: a tip made in IT scores on IT's ladder and never appears on the organisation's. Three separate controls used to gesture at that — a nav dropdown that hid the rooms behind a click, a row of "IT →" buttons that never said which room you were IN, and a sentence on the dashboard card. Three mechanisms for one idea, and none of them answered "where am I, and what else is there" in a single glance. A segmented control answers both at once: every room on screen, the current one filled. Nothing to open, nothing to read twice. Usage — noun names what the page shows, surface says what it is drawn on: {% include "partials/room_switch.html" with noun="ladder" surface="dark" %} {% include "partials/room_switch.html" with noun="round" surface="light" %} `surface` is explicit rather than inherited on purpose. Every invisible-text bug in this codebase came from one set of colours being asked to work on both a cream card and the dark sheet; making the caller state which one it is means the mistake has to be typed rather than merely forgotten. {% endcomment %} {% if current_org.groups_enabled %}
