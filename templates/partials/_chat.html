{% load humanize %} {% comment %} ONE CONVERSATION, DRAWN AS A CONVERSATION. Used by both ends of a message thread — the member's (orgs/member_message_- thread.html) and the admin's (manage/message_thread.html) — so the two cannot drift into being different products. They were: one was a stack of paragraphs with a name above each, the other the same stack with an avatar bolted on, and neither told you who was speaking to whom without reading the names. WHY SIDES. A thread here always has exactly two parties — a member, and the people who run their organisation — so left and right can carry that and the reader stops having to check the name on every bubble. It is the arrangement every messaging app people already use has settled on, which matters more than whether it is objectively best: it is the one nobody has to learn. WHAT A BUBBLE SHOWS, AND WHAT IT DOESN'T. The sender's face and name appear on the FIRST bubble of a run and not on the rest (see _thread_entries: show_head). Four messages from the same person in the same minute is one turn in the conversation; stamping the name on all four makes it read as four separate arrivals. REPLYING TO ONE MESSAGE. Every bubble carries the data a reply needs — its id, its author, a one-line quote — so the composer can be filled in without a round trip. gt-chat.js binds two ways in: the small arrow, and a swipe across the bubble on touch. Both do the same thing, which is what makes the swipe safe to have: nobody has to discover it. Expects: entries (from orgs.views._thread_entries), thread. {% endcomment %}
