The client's note: "a new look and design on the blog timers where we schedule uploads; make that look modern, neat and cool, same with the tags." So they are one panel now, with the scheduler as its own card. The scheduler is the half that earns the redesign: a datetime input tells you the moment you typed, and nothing else. What an editor actually wants to know is the STATE — is this out, is it held, how long until it goes — and the states are not obvious from a date alone, because "published" here means `is_published AND published_at <= now` and a date in the future is a schedule rather than a mistake. {% endcomment %}

{% comment %} THE SCHEDULER. One field still — `published_at` — because that is genuinely all the model has, and adding a second control that means the same thing is how two sources of truth start. What is new is everything around it: * the STATE, said in words and colour, because "published" is not the same as "live" here and the difference had no representation at all; * a countdown on a scheduled story, so "Friday 09:00" is also "in 2 days" without the reader doing calendar arithmetic; * presets, because the four times anybody actually picks are now, this evening, tomorrow morning and next Monday — and typing those into a datetime-local is eight keystrokes and a chance to get the month wrong. No cron anywhere. Every reader-facing query asks for `published_at <= now`, so the moment simply arrives — see admin_panel.models.LivePostManager. The countdown is decoration over a fact the database already enforces. {% endcomment %}
