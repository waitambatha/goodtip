{% comment %} A LIST OF CARDS, NOT A TABLE. The table put four columns on a 640px minimum width inside a panel that is roughly half the page, so it scrolled sideways at every realistic width — and the actions were the column pushed off the edge. What arrived visually was the title, the date and author run together, then a chip, then a row of undifferentiated links: Edit Unpublish Email members Delete. Nothing said which of those was destructive, and the post's own headline competed with its metadata for the same weight. A post is not tabular data. It has one important field — the headline — and everything else is context for it. So: the headline leads at a size you can actually read, the metadata sits under it as one quiet line, the status is a chip on the right where the eye ends, and the actions are grouped with delete separated and coloured as the exception it is. {% endcomment %}
