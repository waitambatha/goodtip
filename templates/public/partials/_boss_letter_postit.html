{% comment %} Presentation A — the post-it note. "This needs updating with the right logo and the right name Good Tip — a more modern look, like a post-it note or the like" (client, Sep 2026). This is the post-it, and it replaces the parchment scroll that used to stand here. WHY A STICKY IS THE RIGHT OBJECT and not just a newer-looking letter: the page is about putting a note in front of somebody who has not asked for it. A sticky is the only piece of stationery whose entire purpose is exactly that, and everyone already knows what finding one on their monitor means. The parchment said "historic document"; this says "read me before your first meeting". HOW IT IS BUILT. A square of colour, tilted two degrees, with three real stickiness cues and nothing else: * the adhesive strip — the top band is very slightly deeper in colour, because that is the part that is glued and it sits a hair off the wall * the lift — the bottom corners curl away, drawn as a shadow that is tight at the top and loose at the bottom rather than an even drop shadow * the tape — one strip of translucent washi across the top-left corner, rotated against the note so it reads as applied rather than printed The second sticky behind it is not decoration: it is what makes the front one read as a pad rather than a painted square, and it is the cheapest depth cue available. Both are aria-hidden, the note itself carries the text. {% endcomment %}
