{% comment %} Home is deliberately absent when you are already on it — a nav item that points at the page you are reading is a dead control, and the wordmark to its left already goes there. Everywhere else it stays, because that is where it is actually useful. SIX ITEMS, NOT TEN (Sep 2026, client). The bar carried every public page there is, which is a site map rather than a menu: at ten items nothing in it stood out, and the row wrapped at the widths most people read the site on. What is left is the six a visitor is actually trying to reach — what this is, what it costs, what is happening, and the two rooms they can look into before joining. The four that came off (Charities, The Good List, About Us, Privacy) are not gone; they are in the footer, which is where a reader goes looking for them. Privacy in particular belongs there — it is the one item nobody navigates to mid-visit and everybody expects to find at the bottom of the page. See _footer.html, where it now has its own legal row. The two auth actions sit in their own group with a divider. They are the only items that change your state rather than move you around, so grouping them by what they DO is more honest than leaving them as the last two of ten links. Each item carries a glyph. Several of these labels are near-neighbours at a glance — News and The Wall are both "somewhere with a list of stuff on it" until you have read them — and the glyph is what tells them apart before you finish. The stylesheet drops the glyphs at the widths where the row has no room for them, so this markup is the full design and the narrow layouts are unchanged. {% endcomment %}
