{% comment %} Home is deliberately absent when you are already on it — a nav item that points at the page you are reading is a dead control, and the wordmark to its left already goes there. Everywhere else it stays, because that is where it is actually useful. PRIVACY IS THE ONLY ONE THAT LEFT (Sep 2026, client). A first pass moved four items to the footer. The client wanted three of them back — The Good List, Charities and About Us are things a visitor goes looking for mid-visit, and the bar is where you look. Privacy is not: it is the one item nobody navigates to while reading, and everybody expects to find it under the copyright. So it stays out of the desktop row and lives in the footer's legal line. It keeps its place in the DRAWER, though. Below 1120px the drawer is not a condensed bar, it is the whole site, and "it's in the footer" is a desktop answer — on a phone that is a long scroll past everything else on the page. The two auth actions sit in their own group with a divider. They are the only items that change your state rather than move you around, so grouping them by what they DO is more honest than leaving them as the last two of ten links. Each item carries a glyph. Several of these labels are near-neighbours at a glance — News and The Wall are both "somewhere with a list of stuff on it" until you have read them — and the glyph is what tells them apart before you finish. The stylesheet drops the glyphs at the widths where the row has no room for them, so this markup is the full design and the narrow layouts are unchanged. {% endcomment %}
